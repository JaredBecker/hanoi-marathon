The plan

    How to run it

    Easy runs

    Conversational pace. Finish feeling like you could keep going.

    Long runs

    Slow and controlled. Practise your run/walk rhythm from the start, not only when tired.

    Marathon pace

    Target cruising pace is around 7:00–7:10/km if sub-5 is on.

    Walking is strategy

    A 9 min run / 1 min walk rhythm can preserve your legs and still keep the average moving.

    Race-day strategy

    0 10 25 32 37 42.2

    1. 0–10 km

      7:00–7:10/km-ish. HOLD BACK. Enjoy the start.

    2. 10–25 km

      Settle in. Run/walk. Fuel regularly. Take in Hanoi.

    3. 25–32 km

      Assess the legs. Stay patient. No heroics.

    4. 32–37 km

      If you're good, gradually push. If not, protect the finish.

    5. 37–42.2 km

      Whatever works: run, walk, dance, high-five strangers. Keep moving.

    Fuel, hydrate, survive

    The boring stuff that gets you to kilometre 42

    Fuel
    30–90 g/hr
    Practise taking carbohydrates during long runs. Start around 30–60 g/hour and build toward roughly 60–90 g/hour if your stomach tolerates it. Use the same products in training that you plan to race with.
    Fluids
    regularly
    Drink regularly rather than waiting until you're thirsty and destroyed. Practise this on long runs, especially in warm conditions.
    The long-run cap
    22–25 km
    A controlled 22–25 km run is enough. You do not need a heroic 30 km training run. Finish healthy rather than proving a point.
    Pain rule
    back off
    Normal fatigue is fine. Persistent, worsening, sharp or altered-gait pain is not. Back off and recover rather than forcing the schedule.
    Tattoo rule
    ~1 week
    For the large bicep tattoo: give it about a week before restarting running, assuming the skin is healing normally and your artist's aftercare instructions agree.

    Take the photo

    The only non-negotiable

    Have a fucking great day.

    This is your first international marathon. If Hanoi has music, dance. If people cheer, high-five them. If you see something amazing, take the photo.

    The clock is the bonus; getting the medal while experiencing the city is the mission.
